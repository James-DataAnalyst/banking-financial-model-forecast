# Methodology

## Modeling principles

The workbook follows four practical principles:

1. **Separate inputs from calculations.** Forecast assumptions are maintained in a dedicated layer rather than embedded throughout output sheets.
2. **Keep outputs traceable.** Dashboard and valuation results are linked back to supporting schedules.
3. **Use connected statements.** Forecast earnings, financial position, and cash flow are treated as parts of one model.
4. **Make uncertainty visible.** Scenario and sensitivity analysis show how the conclusion changes when key assumptions move.

## Analytical workflow

### Historical preparation

- Organize annual income statement, balance-sheet, and cash-flow records.
- Standardize period labels and line-item groupings.
- Calculate trend, growth, and ratio series.
- Review unusual movements before using the history as a forecast baseline.

### Forecast construction

- Define the forecast horizon and fiscal periods.
- Set operating, balance-sheet, capital, and cash-flow drivers.
- Apply driver-based assumptions to the projected periods.
- Connect forecast schedules to the financial statements.
- Monitor model checks, including balance-sheet integrity and non-negative constraints where relevant.

### Banking performance analysis

- Profitability: return on assets, return on equity, margins, and earnings trends.
- Liquidity and funding: current, cash, loan-to-deposit, and related measures.
- Capital: qualifying capital, capital ratios, and risk-weighted assets.
- Asset quality: non-performing loan and related indicators.
- Market measures: earnings per share, valuation multiples, and dividend yield.

### Valuation

- Estimate forecast cash flows from the operating model.
- Calculate the present value of forecast periods.
- Estimate terminal value using a terminal-growth framework.
- Test WACC and terminal-growth combinations in a sensitivity matrix.
- Compare base, bull, and bear assumptions.

### Presentation

- Surface only decision-relevant KPIs on the dashboards.
- Keep detailed schedules available for validation and follow-up analysis.
- Use consistent metric labels and period selections.
- Pair headline outcomes with trend and risk context.

## Important limitations

- The public screenshots are a portfolio demonstration, not a live forecasting system.
- Figures and entity references may be anonymized, modified, or simulated.
- Forecasts are assumption-dependent and do not predict future performance.
- The analysis is not suitable for making financial or investment decisions.
- The underlying workbook and source data are intentionally not distributed in this public package.
