# Case Study: Banking Financial Model, Forecast & Valuation

## Executive summary

This project demonstrates how a banking analysis workflow can be structured in Excel to connect historical performance, forecast assumptions, financial statements, regulatory capital, risk indicators, scenarios, and valuation.

The final solution provides two levels of review:

1. Detailed schedules for analysts who need to inspect assumptions and calculations.
2. Executive dashboards for decision-makers who need a concise view of performance, risk, and outlook.

> **Portfolio disclaimer:** All figures, entity references, assumptions, and outputs in the public presentation have been anonymized, modified, simulated, or retained only for illustrative demonstration. Nothing in this project should be interpreted as actual client information, current market data, or investment advice.

## The challenge

Financial statements are often reviewed independently, even though changes in earnings, assets, funding, capital, liquidity, and cash flow are connected. Forecasting those areas in separate files can create inconsistent assumptions and make it difficult to explain why a valuation or performance conclusion changed.

The objective was to build a transparent Excel model that could:

- organize a long historical record;
- centralize forecast assumptions;
- connect projected statements and cash flows;
- monitor balance and model integrity;
- evaluate profitability, capital, liquidity, leverage, and asset quality;
- compare alternative scenarios; and
- communicate the results through clear dashboards.

## Model design

### 1. Historical layer

Historical income statement, balance-sheet, cash-flow, and market series establish the analytical baseline. Dedicated trend sections make year-on-year movements and longer-term patterns easier to review.

### 2. Assumption layer

Forecast inputs are separated from outputs. The structure supports operating drivers for revenue, expenses, assets, liabilities, and capital, together with checks that flag potential inconsistencies.

### 3. Forecast layer

The forecast schedules translate the selected drivers into projected performance. The model horizon covers five forward periods and connects the projected statements to cash-flow and valuation outputs.

### 4. Risk and capital layer

Bank-specific analysis includes capital adequacy, risk-weighted assets, asset quality, leverage, liquidity, and coverage indicators. This prevents the model from treating earnings growth as the only measure of performance.

### 5. Valuation and scenario layer

The DCF framework discounts forecast cash flows and tests the effect of alternative WACC and terminal-growth assumptions. Base, bull, and bear scenarios make the range of possible outcomes explicit.

### 6. Presentation layer

Two dashboards summarize financial health and strategic outlook. These pages bring together headline metrics, annual trends, rankings, capital indicators, and forecast results without requiring the reader to navigate every supporting schedule.

## Analytical value

The model helps distinguish between several questions that should not be collapsed into one headline number:

- Is profitability improving because of stronger operations or temporary movements?
- Is growth supported by adequate capital and asset quality?
- Are liquidity and leverage developing within acceptable ranges?
- Which assumptions have the greatest effect on forecast value?
- Does a favorable forecast remain credible under a more conservative scenario?

## Deliverables

- Strategic positioning and long-term outlook dashboard
- Financial health dashboard
- Central assumptions and forecast-driver schedule
- Integrated statement and cash-flow forecasts
- DCF valuation and sensitivity table
- Scenario comparison
- Capital adequacy and risk-weighted asset analysis
- Peer profitability ranking
- Historical ratio and market analysis
- Loan amortization schedule

## Result

The finished workbook provides a traceable path from historical performance to forecast assumptions, projected statements, risk metrics, valuation, and executive communication. Its modular design makes it easier to review inputs, challenge assumptions, and understand which drivers change the result.

## What I learned

A strong financial model is not just a collection of formulas. It needs a clear separation between inputs and outputs, checks that protect model integrity, consistent links between the statements, and a presentation layer that explains the result without hiding the assumptions behind it.
